## The business

- **What does the business do?** Sean Kehoe is building a business around the concept of providing personal fitness training to disabled and handicapped people, to give them an option to participate in society better and enable their lives to become better on a personal basis.
- **Who is the typical client?** No typical client — all ages, both sexes, very wide market. Goals are diverse and individualistic, assessed on a needs basis: mobility issues, Special Olympics preparation, general fitness. Location: two types — Sean works out of Point One Fitness on Russell Road (sub-lease/contractual arrangement with the owner), and he does in-home visits plus group sessions outside when appropriate.
- **How does gym delivery work in practice?** For the gym, he rents space/time at Point One Fitness — fully equipped with multiple fitness machines, resistance training devices, workout areas, soft mats. For home visits, he brings portable equipment: dumbbells, terra-bands (elastic stretch resistance bands), sometimes a mat. He uses surroundings — parks, lifting logs. He also does work out of his house with a smaller set of fitness equipment set up for his budding gym business, which he wants to make part of the model.
- **How does personal training fit with the delivery side?** Contractual relationships are usually 1–3 months — clients generally won't engage past that. They buy a set of sessions at a particular session rate, can reschedule with practical limits (details in the documents already prepared for Sean). He has legacy cleanup work to do. No package offerings built yet. Does individual and group sessions (groups up to 6). About 25–30% of business is high school athletes looking for fitness/weight strength training.
