# Incorporation Checklist

## Ownership and Profit Distribution

### For-Profit Corporation:
- Owned by shareholders who buy shares.
- Profits can be handed out to shareholders as cash payments called dividends.
- Designed to enrich the private owners or investors.

### Not-for-Profit Corporation:
- Made up of members rather than shareholders (it is a "non-share capital" structure).
- Members cannot receive financial gain or dividends from the group.
- Any extra money or profit must go right back into the organization to support its mission.

## Model by-laws – Not-for-profit corporations

To be able to issue official donation receipts and to be exempt from taxation, the Income Tax Act requires that corporations created and operated exclusively for charitable purposes register with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) as charities.

Consult "How to draft purposes for charitable registration" to know more.
Charitable purposes of a registered charity - Canada.ca (CRA)

## A purpose charitable at common law must:
- Be for the benefit of the public.

To ensure these requirements are met, a purpose must:
- Define the scope of activities (How)
- Identify, explicitly or implicitly, the eligible beneficiary group (Who)

## The four categories of charity are:
- Advancement of education
- Advancement of religion
- Other purposes beneficial to the community in a way the law regards as charitable

## Appendix A – List of subcategories of other purposes beneficial to the community in a way the law regards as charitable

## Applicable Subcategory:
- Relieve conditions attributable to being a person with a disability

## Eligible beneficiary group (Who)
- Citizens who want to participate in physical activity and have a physical or mental impediment that would prevent participating from doing so in a public setting without adaptive support and the financial means.
